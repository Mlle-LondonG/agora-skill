# 12-week curriculum

Each week trains one competency using the anchor subject's syllabus. Metacognition (predicting, logging errors) runs every week. Phases: **I. System foundations** (W1–W4) · **II. Reasoning** (W5–W8) · **III. Transfer and production** (W9–W12). The weekly rhythm is in SKILL.md §6.4.

## 1. Content per week
| W | Core competency | Learning objective | Suggested materials | Active retrieval | Challenge problem | Realistic case |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deep attention | Sustain focus blocks and map the subject | Syllabus or table of contents and an introductory chapter | Blank-page map of the subject (days 2 and 5) | Fermi-estimate the real hours your goal demands | Plan your real week with 3 likely interruptions and how to contain them |
| 2 | Long-term memory and calibration | Build the card bank and measure calibration | A core chapter with end-of-chapter questions | 20 own questions; predict the % before grading | Rebuild a full procedure or argument from memory | Design a surprise quiz for someone who studied the same: what would you ask and why |
| 3 | First principles | Explain why 3 core ideas work | Texts with derivations; worked examples | A chain of 3 "why?" without looking | Derive or justify a central result without notes | A real error (bug, bad decision, misunderstanding) explained from its root cause |
| 4 | Critical reading of sources | Map thesis, evidence and assumptions | A primary source or paper and one that contradicts it | Argument structure from memory in 5 lines | Find the weakest premise and the data that would refute it | Two conflicting reports: which deserves more weight and why |
| 5 | Logical and causal reasoning | Tell validity, correlation and causation apart | Logic problems; studies with confounders | Inference and fallacy types, with an example of your own | Build a counterexample to a general claim in your subject | Headline "X causes Y": causal diagram, confounders and a design to test it |
| 6 | Probability and decisions under uncertainty | Use base rates, expected value and pre-mortems | Intro to applied probability; a decision case | Key ideas + mid-cycle mini-diagnostic | A base-rate problem solved two ways (formula and natural frequencies) | Harvard-style case: decide with incomplete data, alternatives and a recommendation |
| 7 | Quantitative problem solving | Apply understand–plan–execute–check on interleaved sets | Problem sheets with hidden solutions | Old problems mixed | An interleaved set of 3 types without saying which is which | Estimate a real quantity in your surroundings with data and check it against a source |
| 8 | Argumentative writing and oral defence | Write and defend an essay with a debatable thesis | 2–3 quality sources on a disputed question | Essay outline from memory | Answer the strongest objection in writing | Simulated supervision: defend the essay under questioning |
| 9 | Combinatorial creativity and hypotheses | Generate alternatives before converging | Solutions from other fields; design cases | Principles of the subject learned so far | 15 hypotheses or solutions; pick 2 with explicit criteria and test 1 | Design challenge: improve a real process or product for a specific user |
| 10 | Transfer | Apply principles to problems that look different | Problems from other domains with the same deep structure | Full interleaving of the whole cycle | Solve a problem from another domain with a principle from your subject | A case from another field (health, law, engineering) analysed with your tools |
| 11 | Integration: project | Research, produce and get feedback on a real product | Whatever the project needs (`projects.md`) | Daily review of everything pending | The project's hardest technical milestone | The project itself, before a real user or reader |
| 12 | Defence and final metacognition | Defend the project, repeat the diagnostic, plan the next cycle | Your 12 weeks of work | Final diagnostic in a parallel form | An unanticipated defence question | 12-week review with data: what worked and what didn't |

## 2. Written work, supervision and pass criteria
| W | Written work | Socratic session (day 6) | Weekly metric | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What I know, what I don't, and how I'll find out" (300 words) | Defend that your goal is observable and achievable | Uninterrupted focus minutes; sessions completed | ≥5 sessions; notebook started; ≥1 uninterrupted 25-min block |
| 2 | Explain a core concept without notes (400) | The tutor probes the gap between what you think you know and what you recall | 24-h recall %; calibration gap | Delayed recall ≥70 %; bank ≥30 cards |
| 3 | First-principles explanation (500) | A chain of "why?" down to what you cannot justify | Explanation rubric | ≥2.5/4 on the mechanism criterion |
| 4 | Critical review of a source (600) | Defend your evaluation of the evidence against objections | Essay rubric (evidence) | Names the thesis, 3 assumptions and 1 real limitation; essay ≥2.5 |
| 5 | Causal analysis of a claim (600) | Counterexamples to your general claims | % logic items correct; question quality | ≥80 % on the logic set; ≥1 valid counterexample of your own |
| 6 | Decision memo (1 page) | Sceptical committee: defend the recommendation | Calibration; mini-diagnostic vs initial | Memo ≥3/4; calibration gap ≤15 points |
| 7 | Annotated solutions to 2 hard problems | Justify the choice of each strategy | Valid solutions per level; minutes to a valid solution | ≥2 valid L3 solutions; median L2 time lower than in W3 |
| 8 | Argumentative essay (800–1500) | Cambridge-style supervision | Essay rubric | ≥3/4 on thesis and counter-argument |
| 9 | Proposal with discarded alternatives (500) | Defend why you discarded the other options | Distinct alternatives; question quality | ≥15 ideas, 2 explicit criteria, 1 test run |
| 10 | The same principle in 2 domains (600) | New problems without being told which principle applies | Transfer rubric | ≥3/4 on 2 of 3 L4 problems |
| 11 | Progress report + log of feedback received | Project design review | Milestones done; changes made from feedback | Version 1 delivered and ≥1 feedback round incorporated |
| 12 | Final report and data-based reflection (800) | Final defence | Final vs initial diagnostic; 12-week metrics | Project ≥3/4 with no criterion <2; next-cycle plan written |

If a criterion is missed, apply SKILL.md §8. A week may be passed with one pending criterion only if it is logged as a reinforcement for the next week; with two pending, repeat the week with new problems.

## 3. Adapting to available time
**General scale**

| Element | 30 min | 60 min | 120 min | 180 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval questions | 5 | 8–10 | 12–15 | 15–20 |
| Problems per session | 1 | 2–3 | 4–6 (≥1 L3) | 6–10 (≥2 L3, 1 L4) |
| Weekly written work | ≈40 % of stated length | ≈70 % | 100 % | 100 % + revised version |
| Case | Every 2 weeks, 10-min version | Weekly, 20 min | Weekly, full | Weekly + written memo |
| Supervision (day 6) | 30 min | 45 min | 60 min | 75–90 min |
| Interleaving | From W3 | From W2 | From W2 | From W1 if there are 2 subjects |

**Week by week: what to prioritise**

| W | 30 min | 60 min | 120 min | 180 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Simple map; 120-word text | Map and full text | Map with dependencies; full case | Plus a plan with 2 load scenarios |
| 2 | 10 cards; daily prediction | 20 cards | 30 cards; full procedure | Plus a quiz for a peer |
| 3 | 1 core idea explained | 2 ideas | 3 ideas and one derivation | Plus a second derivation by another route |
| 4 | Source excerpt; 3 assumptions | Whole source | Source and counter-source | Plus a third source from another school |
| 5 | 4 logic items a day | 8 items | Set and causal diagram | Plus an experiment design |
| 6 | Half-page memo | 1-page memo | Memo and decision tree | Plus sensitivity analysis |
| 7 | 1 interleaved problem | 3 problems | 5 problems and 1 L3 | 8 problems, 2 L3 and 1 L4 |
| 8 | 350-word essay | 600 words | 1000 words | 1500 words and version 2 |
| 9 | 8 ideas and 1 criterion | 15 ideas | 15 ideas and a test | Plus a test with 3 users |
| 10 | 1 L4 problem a week | 2 | 3 | 5 |
| 11 | Minimum viable project | Small project | Full project | Full project and 2 feedback rounds |
| 12 | Diagnostic over 3 days | Diagnostic in 2 parts | Full diagnostic | Plus a public presentation |

## 4. Level modifiers
| Element | Foundations | Intermediate | Advanced | Intensive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Writing | Structured paragraphs | Short essay | Full essay | Essay + written reply to objections |
| Interleaving | Only after mastering each block | 2 topics | 3 topics | 3+ topics and subjects |
| Pass criteria | §2 thresholds minus 10 points (on 0–4 scales, minus 0.5) | As in §2 | As in §2 | Thresholds plus 5 points (on 0–4 scales, plus 0.25) |

Problem levels, hint limits and minimum attempt times: SKILL.md §7.

## 5. Language anchor subjects
When the anchor subject is a language, map the weeks like this: W1 attention with graded input · W2 card bank of full sentences · W3 why a structure works (e.g. aspect, agreement) · W4 critical reading of an authentic text · W5 logic of connectors and argument · W6 deciding in the language (negotiation, choosing options) · W7 accuracy drills under time · W8 essay + oral defence · W9 creative writing or storytelling · W10 switching register and context · W11–W12 a real-world project (podcast, presentation, interview). Track errors per 100 words, words per minute and % of sentences recalled.
